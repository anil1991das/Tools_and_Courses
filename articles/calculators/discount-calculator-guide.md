# How to Calculate Discounts and Final Sale Prices

Discounts are common in online shopping, retail stores, subscriptions, travel bookings, and business quotations. A percentage discount reduces the marked or original price by a specified proportion.

Knowing how to calculate the saving and final price helps compare offers and avoid being misled by promotional labels. The [iamnoobs Discount Calculator](https://iamnoobs.com/discount-calculator) helps calculate common discount values from a price and percentage.

## What is a discount?

A discount is a reduction from an original or listed price.

For example, if a product costs ₹2,000 and the seller offers a 15% discount, the discount amount is calculated from the original price.

~~~text
Discount amount = Original price × Discount rate / 100
~~~

The final price is:

~~~text
Final price = Original price - Discount amount
~~~

## Example: calculate a 15% discount

Original price: ₹2,000

Discount rate: 15%

~~~text
Discount amount = 2,000 × 15 / 100
                = ₹300

Final price = 2,000 - 300
            = ₹1,700
~~~

The customer saves ₹300 and pays ₹1,700 before any additional taxes or fees.

## Calculate the discount percentage

Sometimes you know the original price and sale price but want to find the discount percentage.

Use:

~~~text
Discount percentage =
((Original price - Sale price) / Original price) × 100
~~~

Example:

- Original price: ₹1,500
- Sale price: ₹1,200
- Saving: ₹300

~~~text
(300 / 1,500) × 100 = 20%
~~~

The discount is 20%.

## Understand successive discounts

A common promotion applies one discount and then another discount to the reduced amount.

For example, a product priced at ₹1,000 receives a 20% discount followed by a 10% discount.

~~~text
After first discount:
1,000 × 0.80 = 800

After second discount:
800 × 0.90 = 720
~~~

The final price is ₹720. The total saving is ₹280, which is 28% of the original price, not 30%.

The second discount is calculated on the already reduced amount.

## Discount versus cashback

A discount reduces the amount payable at the point of sale. Cashback is generally credited or returned later, subject to the offer's conditions.

A cashback promotion may have a minimum transaction, maximum benefit, eligible payment method, expiry date, or delayed credit. Do not treat cashback as an immediate price reduction unless the terms clearly support that interpretation.

## Compare offers correctly

Before deciding that one offer is better, compare:

- Final amount payable.
- Delivery or convenience charges.
- Applicable taxes.
- Minimum purchase conditions.
- Maximum discount limits.
- Payment-method restrictions.
- Cashback eligibility and timing.
- Return and cancellation rules.

A higher advertised percentage does not always lead to a lower final price.

## Common discount calculation mistakes

### 1. Applying the percentage to the wrong amount

Use the original price as the base for a single discount. For successive discounts, apply each discount to the current reduced amount.

### 2. Adding successive discount percentages

A 20% discount followed by 10% is not a 30% discount. The second reduction applies to the new price.

### 3. Ignoring fees and taxes

The displayed sale price may exclude shipping, convenience charges, or applicable taxes. Check the final checkout amount.

### 4. Confusing savings with final price

The discount amount is what you save. The final price is what you pay before any additional charges.

### 5. Trusting the marked price without comparison

A promotion's original price may not represent the usual market price. Compare the actual product and seller rather than relying only on the percentage label.

## Frequently asked questions

### How do I calculate 10% off a price?

Multiply the original price by 10 and divide by 100. Subtract the result from the original price to get the discounted amount.

### Is a 50% discount followed by 50% another 100% discount?

No. A 50% discount followed by another 50% reduces the remaining price by half again. The total reduction is 75% of the original price.

### Can a discount be more than 100%?

A discount above 100% would make the calculated price negative under the simple formula. Such an input is generally not a normal retail discount and should be checked.
