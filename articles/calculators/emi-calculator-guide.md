# How to Calculate Loan EMI: Formula and Examples

When taking a home loan, car loan, personal loan, or another instalment-based loan, one of the first questions is: how much will the monthly payment be?

An Equated Monthly Instalment (EMI) is a fixed periodic payment that generally includes both principal repayment and interest for a reducing-balance loan with a fixed rate and regular monthly periods.

## What is EMI?

EMI stands for Equated Monthly Instalment. Under a standard reducing-balance repayment schedule, each payment covers:

- Interest due for the period.
- A portion of the outstanding principal.

Early payments generally contain a larger interest component. As the outstanding principal reduces, the interest portion decreases and more of the EMI goes toward principal repayment.

The EMI amount can remain constant when the interest rate and other repayment assumptions remain unchanged.

## EMI formula

For a standard fixed-rate monthly repayment, the EMI formula is:

~~~text
EMI = P × r × (1 + r)^n / ((1 + r)^n - 1)
~~~

Where:

- P is the principal loan amount.
- r is the monthly interest rate expressed as a decimal.
- n is the total number of monthly instalments.

If the annual interest rate is quoted as a percentage, a common nominal monthly conversion is:

~~~text
r = Annual interest rate / 12 / 100
~~~

The exact treatment of rates and compounding should match the lender's loan agreement.

## Example EMI calculation

Suppose a loan has these illustrative inputs:

| Input | Value |
|---|---:|
| Principal | ₹5,00,000 |
| Annual interest rate | 12% |
| Tenure | 5 years |
| Number of monthly instalments | 60 |

Monthly rate:

~~~text
r = 12 / 12 / 100
  = 0.01
~~~

Number of instalments:

~~~text
n = 5 × 12
  = 60
~~~

Substituting the values into the EMI formula gives an estimated monthly instalment of approximately ₹11,122.

The actual lender amount can differ because of rate conventions, disbursement timing, fees, insurance, payment dates, and lender-specific rounding.

## How tenure changes EMI

For the same principal and interest rate:

- A longer tenure generally reduces the monthly EMI.
- A longer tenure usually increases the total interest paid.
- A shorter tenure generally increases the EMI but reduces the time over which interest accrues.

A lower EMI is not necessarily a cheaper loan. Compare both the monthly payment and total repayment.

## Reducing-balance interest

Under a reducing-balance method, interest for a period is calculated on the outstanding principal rather than the original loan amount.

At the beginning of repayment, the outstanding balance is high, so the interest component is larger. Later, as principal is repaid, interest reduces.

A repayment schedule can help show this movement month by month.

## What an EMI estimate may not include

A simple calculator generally focuses on principal, rate, and tenure. Your actual loan cost may also include:

- Processing fees.
- Documentation or administrative charges.
- Insurance premiums, if applicable.
- Late-payment charges.
- Prepayment or foreclosure conditions.
- Floating-rate changes.
- Taxes or other applicable charges.

Read the lender's Key Facts Statement and loan agreement for the actual cost and repayment conditions.

## Fixed versus floating interest rates

A fixed-rate loan keeps the applicable interest rate unchanged for the agreed period, subject to the contract.

A floating-rate loan can change when the benchmark or lender's applicable rate changes. This may change the EMI, repayment tenure, or both, depending on the lender's policy and agreement.

A calculator using one constant rate provides an estimate, not a forecast of future floating rates.

## Common EMI calculation mistakes

### 1. Entering annual interest as monthly interest

If the calculator expects an annual rate percentage, enter it as stated. Do not divide by 12 unless the input instructions require a monthly rate.

### 2. Mixing years and months

A tenure of five years is 60 monthly instalments. Confirm the calculator's tenure unit.

### 3. Looking only at EMI

Compare total interest and total repayment as well. A smaller monthly instalment over a much longer period can cost more overall.

### 4. Ignoring additional charges

Fees and insurance can change the effective borrowing cost. Check the lender's actual disclosures.

## Frequently asked questions

### Does EMI remain the same every month?

For a standard fixed-rate loan with regular monthly instalments, the EMI is generally constant. Floating-rate changes or loan restructuring can alter the amount or tenure.

### Does a longer tenure reduce total interest?

Usually not. A longer tenure generally reduces the monthly payment but increases the total interest paid when the rate and principal are otherwise comparable.

### Is an EMI calculator an approval check?

No. It estimates repayment based on inputs. Loan approval depends on the lender's eligibility, credit assessment, income, obligations, and policies.
